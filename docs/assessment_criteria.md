# GLEAM Lens: Youth Dashboard Challenge 2027 – Assessment Criteria

[← Back to the call](../README.md) · [PDF version](GLEAM_Lens_Assessment_Criteria.pdf)

## Overview of the selection process

| Stage | When | Who assesses | Method | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Round 1a: Eligibility screening** | 16 – 23 Mar 2027 | Secretariat (GLEAM team) | Pass/fail eligibility, completeness and “demo runs” checks | Eligible demos go to the jury |
| **Round 1b: Demo review** | 24 Mar – 7 Apr 2027 | Jury | Four-criteria rubric applied to the clickable demo and design concept, 0–100 points | **5 finalists**, announced by 9 Apr 2027 |
| **Round 2a: Final screening** | 16 – 23 Jun 2027 | Secretariat (GLEAM team) | Pass/fail compliance and data checks | Eligible finals go to the jury |
| **Round 2b: Final review** | 24 Jun – 16 Jul 2027 (online presentations mid-July) | Jury | 70% rubric on the revised app, taking response to mentorship into account + 30% online presentation | **One winning team**, notified by 31 Jul; award at the Global Conference, Sep 2027 |

## Scoring rubric (both rounds)

Both rounds use the same four criteria and weights. Round 1 applies them to the clickable demo; Round 2 applies them to the revised app.

Each juror scores each criterion from **1 to 5** using the descriptors below. The weighted score is **Σ (score ÷ 5 × weight)**, giving 0–100 points.

| Criterion | Weight | Key question | Score 1 (weak) | Score 3 (adequate) | Score 5 (excellent) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Integration of emissions and water | 25% | Do emissions and water feel like one product rather than two dashboards placed side by side? | Two separate sections; no shared filters or links between themes | Shared navigation and filters; some combined views | Seamless experience; shared filters carry across; insightful combined views (e.g. emission and water intensity per kg protein) that reveal trade-offs and co-benefits |
| 2. User-centred design and storytelling | 25% | Are real users and tasks driving the design? | No clear users; data dump | Personas defined; main tasks reachable, with some friction | Evidence-based personas; key insights in 1–3 clicks; clear narrative that guides different users |
| 3. Usability and accessibility | 25% | Can a broad audience use it on any device? | Jargon, poor contrast, desktop only | Plain labels; works on mobile with minor issues | Plain language with glossary; colour-blind-safe palette; responsive; light on bandwidth; keyboard and screen-reader friendly |
| 4. Technical feasibility and handover | 25% | Could FAO realistically build or adopt this? | App breaks or does not run from the README; undocumented code | Runs reliably; reasonable code structure and README | Clean, documented code (e.g. modular structure, clear logic, open packages); deploys reliably; easy to maintain and extend with new GLEAM versions |

Scores of 2 and 4 are used for entries that fall between descriptors.

## Response to mentorship (Round 2)

Round 2 rewards teams that learn from GLEAM mentorship. There is no separate weight; instead, response to mentorship is built into each criterion:

1. Jurors receive each finalist’s Round 1 demo, the written jury feedback and a short summary from the team’s mentor of the main recommendations.
2. For each criterion, jurors check whether the relevant feedback was addressed, either by a change in the app or by a convincing explanation in the design rationale.
3. A criterion where key feedback was ignored without explanation cannot score above **3**.
4. Clear, well-judged improvements since Round 1 support a score of **4 or 5**.

## Procedure

- **Round 1:** each demo is scored independently by **two jurors**; the entry score is the average. If the two scores differ by **more than 20 points**, a third juror scores the entry and the two closest scores are averaged. A calibration meeting confirms the 5 finalists.
- **Round 2:** every juror scores every finalist; the rubric score is the average across jurors.

## Final score and winner

| Component | Weight | How it is scored |
|---|---|---|
| Revised dashboard | 70% | Scoring rubric above (0–100), including response to mentorship; average of all jurors |
| Presentation: clarity of story | 10% | 1–5: clear problem, users and value |
| Presentation: live demonstration | 10% | 1–5: main user journey shown working end to end |
| Presentation: Q&A and understanding | 10% | 1–5: confident, accurate answers; team understands its own code and data, including AI-assisted parts |

**Final score** = 0.7 × rubric score + 0.3 × presentation score (both on 0–100). The team with the highest final score is the **winner**. Round 1 scores do not carry over.

### Tie-break

1. Higher score on User-centred design and storytelling.
2. Higher score on Integration of emissions and water.
3. Decision by the jury Chair.
